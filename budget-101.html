Budget 101: The 50/30/20 Rule

A simple way to split your paycheck without spreadsheets or willpower.

What Is the 50/30/20 Rule?

The 50/30/20 rule is a beginner-friendly budgeting framework popularized by U.S. Senator Elizabeth Warren in her book "All Your Worth: The Ultimate Lifetime Money Plan." It splits your after-tax (take-home) income into three buckets, giving you a rough but effective guide for where your money should go every month.

50% Needs
30% Wants
20% Savings

Breaking Down the Three Buckets

50% — NEEDS

Essential expenses you can't reasonably avoid.

  • Rent or mortgage
  • Groceries
  • Utilities & phone bill
  • Insurance premiums
  • Minimum debt payments
  • Transportation to work

30% — WANTS

Things that improve quality of life, but aren't strictly necessary.

  • Dining out & takeout
  • Streaming subscriptions
  • Hobbies & entertainment
  • Travel & vacations
  • Shopping beyond basics
  • Upgraded gadgets

20% — SAVINGS & DEBT

Paying your future self first.

  • Emergency fund
  • Retirement contributions
  • Investing (index funds, etc.)
  • Extra debt payments (beyond minimum)
  • Big savings goals (house, car)

A Worked Example

Say your monthly take-home pay is ₹60,000. Here's how the split would look:

Category Percentage Amount
Needs50%₹30,000
Wants30%₹18,000
Savings & Debt20%₹12,000
Total100%₹60,000

How to Set It Up in 4 Steps

  1. Calculate your after-tax income. Use your actual take-home pay — after tax, insurance, and any automatic deductions — not your gross salary.
  2. Track your current spending for a month. Categorize every expense as a Need, Want, or Saving so you know your real starting point before changing anything.
  3. Compare against the 50/30/20 targets. If "Needs" is eating up 70% of your income, the fix usually isn't more willpower — it's reducing fixed costs (rent, subscriptions) or increasing income.
  4. Automate the split. Set up automatic transfers so your "Savings" bucket moves to a separate account the day you get paid — before you have a chance to spend it.
Pro tip: Automate savings first, budget the rest second. If your 20% savings transfer happens automatically on payday, you never have to rely on discipline to "remember" to save what's left over — because there's nothing left to accidentally spend.
When it doesn't fit perfectly: In high cost-of-living cities, "Needs" can easily exceed 50% of income even after cutting corners. In that case, treat 50/30/20 as a directional guide rather than a strict law — the real goal is intentionality, not the exact percentages. Adjust to something realistic like 60/20/20 and aim to shift the ratio over time as income grows.

Why This Framework Works

The 50/30/20 rule works because it's simple enough to actually stick to. Most detailed budgeting systems fail not because the math is wrong, but because they require too much ongoing effort to track dozens of micro-categories. This rule gives you just enough structure to catch overspending early, while still leaving room for guilt-free spending on things you enjoy.
